# Target Card: BAFF receptor / TNFRSF13C

## Summary
- Disease area: Sjögren disease, SLE/LN, systemic sclerosis B-cell-high subgroup.
- Target: BAFF-R / TNFRSF13C, UniProt Q96RJ3.
- Proposed modality: Fc-silent BAFF-R antagonist or calibrated Fc-effector antibody.
- Recommendation: High.
- Thesis: BAFF-R offers a more receptor-selective B-cell survival intervention than soluble BAFF blockade, with room for Fc-tuned differentiation.
- Last landscape check: 2026-07-05.

## Scoring
| Axis | Score | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Unmet need | 5 | Sjögren and SLE have persistent steroid/immunosuppressant dependence. |
| Target biology | 4 | BAFF-R supports autoreactive mature B-cell survival. |
| Antibody tractability | 5 | Cell-surface receptor on B cells. |
| Differentiation | 4 | Blocking-only versus depletion can be engineered. |
| Safety window | 3 | Humoral immunity suppression is expected. |
| Biomarker strategy | 4 | BAFF, B-cell subsets, IgG, CXCL13, autoantibodies. |
| Developability | 4 | Conventional IgG feasible, with receptor occupancy/internalization questions. |

## Existing landscape
Ianalumab/VAY736 is in phase 3 Sjögren and SLE extension studies and phase 2 systemic sclerosis per ClinicalTrials.gov. Belimumab validates BAFF biology but does not directly target BAFF-R.

## Antibody rationale
BAFF-R antibody can block ligand binding and optionally deplete BAFF-R-positive B cells. Fc-silent design may suit chronic Sjögren; Fc-enhanced design may suit severe B-cell-high disease but raises infection/hypogammaglobulinemia risk.

## Risks
- Biology risk: plasma cells and APRIL/BCMA survival may bypass BAFF-R.
- Safety risk: infections, hypogammaglobulinemia, poor vaccine responses.
- Competitive risk: ianalumab is advanced.
- Patient selection risk: BAFF-high does not always equal BAFF-R-dependent.

## Kill criteria
- No differentiation versus belimumab/ianalumab in B-cell survival, tissue B-cell or biomarker assays.
- Fc-silent format requires impractical exposure; Fc-active format causes excessive depletion/hypo-Ig.
- Human disease tissue shows low BAFF-R-positive pathogenic B-cell contribution.

## Key experiments
BAFF competition and receptor occupancy; primary B-cell survival and plasmablast differentiation; FcγR/ADCC tuning panel; Sjögren salivary gland and SLE blood biomarker stratification; repeat-dose B-cell/Ig modeling.
