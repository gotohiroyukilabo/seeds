# Target Card: OSM receptor / OSMR

## Summary
- Disease area: Systemic sclerosis, hidradenitis suppurativa, fibrotic/itchy inflammatory skin disease.
- Target: OSMR, UniProt Q99650.
- Proposed modality: Fc-silent blocking antibody; masked antibody for inflamed/fibrotic tissue if systemic repair risk is high.
- Recommendation: High.
- Thesis: OSMR is attractive because it targets tissue remodeling and stromal inflammation rather than broad immune suppression, but safety around repair biology is the gating issue.
- Last landscape check: 2026-07-05.

## Existing landscape
No approved OSMR antibody identified in this check. OSM/OSMR axis appears less clinically crowded than BAFF, CD40L, IL-6 or FcRn.

## Antibody rationale
Blocking receptor signaling can directly modulate fibroblast, keratinocyte and epithelial inflammatory remodeling. Fc-silent design avoids killing OSMR-expressing structural cells.

## Risks
- Biology risk: OSMR may be a consequence rather than driver.
- Safety risk: impaired wound healing, epithelial repair, infection/barrier defects.
- Patient selection risk: lesional OSMR-high signature may not translate into response.
- Developability risk: cynomolgus cross-reactivity and receptor-complex pharmacology unknown.

## Kill criteria
- OSMR blockade fails in patient fibroblast/keratinocyte explants or organoids.
- Normal tissue repair assays show unacceptable impairment at efficacious concentrations.
- Target expression is too broad for systemic chronic dosing without masking.
- No feasible biomarker threshold for OSMR/OSM-high disease.

## Key experiments
Single-cell and spatial expression in SSc/HS lesions; OSM-induced fibroblast/keratinocyte cytokine and matrix assays; wound scratch and epithelial repair safety assays; masked protease-activation feasibility in inflamed skin/fibrotic tissue.
