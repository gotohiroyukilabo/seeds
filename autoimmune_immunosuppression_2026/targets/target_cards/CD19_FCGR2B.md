# Target Card: CD19 x FcGR2B inhibitory B-cell bispecific

## Summary
- Disease area: IgG4-RD, Sjögren disease, SLE, autoimmune cytopenias, pemphigus-like autoantibody disease.
- Targets: CD19 UniProt P15391; FcGR2B UniProt P31994.
- Proposed modality: Non-cytolytic bispecific antibody co-ligating CD19 and inhibitory FcγRIIb.
- Recommendation: High.
- Recommendation after obexelimab differentiation review: Medium-Low unless a concrete non-obexelimab wedge is demonstrated.
- Thesis: B-cell inhibition without depletion could create a safer chronic autoimmune alternative to CD19/CD20 cytotoxic antibodies, but the CD19 x FcGR2B concept itself is already clinically occupied by obexelimab.
- Last landscape check: 2026-07-05.

## Existing landscape
Obexelimab is an experimental CD19/FCGR2B bifunctional antibody. ClinicalTrials.gov lists phase 3 IgG4-RD and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia, plus phase 2 SLE/MS studies.

## Antibody rationale
The antibody is the mechanism: CD19 localizes the molecule to B-lineage cells and the BCR/CD19 co-receptor activation context, while FcγRIIb supplies an ITIM/SHIP inhibitory brake. A bispecific can create cis co-engagement on the same B cell, which is expected to suppress BCR-driven Ca flux, antigen uptake, antigen presentation, T-cell help, germinal-center responses and plasmablast/antibody production. This spatial co-engagement is not easily replicated by small molecules or by simply dosing separate anti-CD19 and anti-FcγRIIb antibodies.

## Risks
- Biology risk: inhibition may be insufficient in severe organ-threatening disease.
- Safety risk: myeloid FcγR biology, immune-complex effects, infection if B-cell function too suppressed.
- Competitive risk: obexelimab is already advanced in IgG4-RD, wAIHA, SLE and MS; inebilizumab/Uplizna is already approved in IgG4-RD. FTO and differentiation are likely the gating risks, not secondary risks.
- Developability risk: bispecific geometry and target-density dependence.

## Kill criteria
- No inhibition of BCR-induced activation, plasmablast differentiation or autoantibody production in primary human cells.
- Requires target expression density absent in intended patient segment.
- Depletion or cytokine activation appears despite intended non-cytolytic design.
- No FTO/differentiation path versus obexelimab-like format.
- No measurable advantage over an obexelimab-like comparator in BCR signaling, antigen uptake/presentation, GC-like B-cell/T-cell co-culture, dosing burden, or biomarker-defined patient segment.

## Key experiments
CD19/FCGR2B co-engagement geometry screen; FcγRIIb ITIM phosphorylation and SHIP pathway engagement; BCR-induced Ca flux; BCR-mediated antigen uptake; B-cell antigen presentation to T cells; plasmablast differentiation and Ig secretion; immune complex and FcγR cross-reactivity panel; IgG4-RD plasmablast ex vivo inhibition; non-depleting safety assay with NK/macrophage effector cells.
