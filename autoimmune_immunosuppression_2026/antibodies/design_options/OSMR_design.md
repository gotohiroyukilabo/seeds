# OSMR Antibody Design Options

## Recommended direction
Prioritize an Fc-silent receptor blocker, then evaluate a masked version if normal repair assays show a narrow safety margin.

## Blocking antibody
- MoA: block OSMR receptor complex signaling and downstream stromal/epithelial inflammatory remodeling.
- Benefit: targets tissue remodeling rather than broad immune suppression.
- Differentiation: less crowded than TNF/IL-17/B-cell axes in fibrotic skin and HS-like disease.
- Safety: impaired wound healing, epithelial repair defects, infection/barrier issues.
- Developability: receptor complex pharmacology, cross-reactivity.
- Assays: OSM-induced STAT3/cytokine/matrix assays in fibroblasts and keratinocytes.
- Kill criteria: no patient-tissue suppression or unacceptable repair impairment.

## Fc-silent antibody
- MoA: functional blockade without ADCC/CDC against OSMR-positive structural cells.
- Benefit: avoids depletion of normal fibroblasts/epithelial cells.
- Risk: insufficient efficacy if pathogenic cells need removal.
- Kill criteria: any Fc-mediated structural-cell killing.

## Masked antibody
- MoA: protease-activated OSMR blocker in inflamed/fibrotic tissue.
- Benefit: may widen safety window for systemic dosing.
- Risk: off-tissue activation, weak recovered potency.
- Assays: plasma stability, lesional protease cleavage, normal skin/lung repair assays.
- Kill criteria: activation in normal plasma or no activation in target lesions.

## Bispecific antibody
Medium priority. Pair OSMR with IL-36R/IL-1 axis in HS or with CCL24/TL1A in fibrosis only if ex vivo combination data justify complexity.

## ADC
Not recommended. Depleting OSMR-positive structural cells is likely unsafe for chronic autoimmune/fibrotic indications.

## pH-dependent recycling antibody
Low-medium priority for dosing convenience; not core to safety or biology.
