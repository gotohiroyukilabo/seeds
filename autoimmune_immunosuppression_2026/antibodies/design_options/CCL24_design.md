# CCL24 Antibody Design Options

## Recommended direction
Start with high-potency neutralization and engineer pH-dependent recycling early if antigen sink or dosing looks limiting.

## Blocking antibody
- MoA: neutralize CCL24 and reduce CCR3-mediated eosinophil/monocyte trafficking and fibroinflammatory signaling.
- Benefit: extracellular soluble target; clean antibody access.
- Differentiation: less crowded fibrosis chemokine axis.
- Safety: altered eosinophil trafficking/host defense.
- Developability: chemokine redundancy and soluble antigen sink.
- Assays: CCR3 chemotaxis, fibroblast co-culture, SSc/PSC explants.
- Kill criteria: no CCL24-high patient segment or no functional effect in patient tissue.

## pH-dependent recycling antibody
- MoA: bind CCL24 in plasma/tissue, release in acidic endosome, recycle antibody and enhance antigen clearance.
- Benefit: better dosing and antigen-sink handling.
- Differentiation: practical improvement over conventional neutralizing IgG.
- Risk: pH tuning can reduce potency.
- Kill criteria: no PK/PD advantage in antigen-rich model.

## Bispecific antibody
Consider only after redundancy mapping. Candidate pairings: CCL11/CCL26, OSM, or another fibrosis mediator. Fixed stoichiometry and broad chemokine suppression are concerns.

## Fc-silent / Fc-enhanced
Fc-silent is acceptable but not central; Fc-enhanced is not useful for a soluble chemokine and may increase immune-complex risk.

## ADC
Not suitable because CCL24 is soluble and does not provide selective internalization into pathogenic cells.

## Masked antibody
Lower priority. Masked soluble-ligand neutralization is possible but adds complexity unless systemic CCL24 blockade proves unsafe.
