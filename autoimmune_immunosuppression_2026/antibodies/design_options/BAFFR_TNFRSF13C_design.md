# BAFF-R / TNFRSF13C Antibody Design Options

## Recommended direction
Develop two parallel concepts early: Fc-silent antagonist for chronic Sjögren/SLE, and calibrated Fc-active depletion for severe B-cell-high disease. Drop the Fc-active arm quickly if it does not beat CD20/CD19 logic.

## Blocking antibody
- MoA: prevent BAFF binding to BAFF-R.
- Benefit: receptor-selective modulation of autoreactive mature B-cell survival.
- Differentiation: more selective than soluble BAFF neutralization.
- Safety: infection, hypo-Ig, vaccine impairment.
- Developability: receptor occupancy, internalization, ligand competition.
- Assays: BAFF competition, B-cell survival, plasmablast differentiation.
- Kill criteria: no advantage over belimumab-like ligand blockade.

## Fc-silent antibody
- MoA: antagonism without ADCC/CDC.
- Benefit: chronic safety and reversibility.
- Differentiation: separates BAFF-R blockade from depletion.
- Risk: insufficient efficacy in high-burden disease.
- Kill criteria: requires exposure incompatible with monthly/subcutaneous dosing.

## Fc-enhanced antibody
- MoA: deplete BAFF-R-positive B cells while blocking survival.
- Benefit: deeper reset than ligand blockade.
- Safety: hypogammaglobulinemia/infection.
- Kill criteria: depletion profile not meaningfully better than CD20/CD19 or too broad.

## Bispecific antibody
Possible but not first-line. Pairing BAFF-R with FcGR2B or tissue-homing concepts is attractive, but CMC/geometry complexity needs a clear patient-selection advantage.

## ADC
Not recommended for chronic autoimmunity because B-cell aplasia and payload toxicity likely narrow the therapeutic index.

## Masked / pH-dependent
Masked antibody is medium-low priority unless systemic B-cell effects limit dose. pH-dependent binding may help receptor/ligand handling but is not the primary differentiation.
