# CD19 x FcGR2B Antibody Design Options

## Recommended direction
Prioritize a non-cytolytic, Fc-silent bispecific that co-ligates CD19 and FcγRIIb. This is the design, not an optional embellishment.

## Bispecific antibody
- MoA: bring inhibitory FcγRIIb to CD19-positive B cells/plasmablasts to dampen BCR signaling and antibody production.
- Benefit: suppresses pathogenic B-cell activity without full depletion.
- Differentiation: safer maintenance than CD19/CD20 depletion in IgG4-RD, Sjögren, SLE.
- Safety: immune-complex effects, myeloid FcγR engagement, infection.
- Developability: geometry, valency, target-density dependence.
- Assays: calcium flux, pSYK/BTK, plasmablast differentiation, Ig secretion, cytokine release.
- Kill criteria: no inhibitory signaling in primary patient B cells, or evidence of depletion/cytokine activation.

## Fc-silent antibody
- MoA: remove Fc effector functions so activity is driven by CD19-FcGR2B co-ligation.
- Benefit: avoids cytotoxic B-cell depletion.
- Risk: lower efficacy in organ-threatening disease.
- Kill criteria: disease-relevant assays require ADCC/CDC to work.

## Blocking antibody / agonistic antibody
CD19-only blocking has weak rationale. FcGR2B agonism without B-cell targeting may affect myeloid cells and immune complexes unpredictably.

## ADC
Not recommended. CD19 ADCs create oncology-like payload and depletion liabilities that are poorly suited for chronic autoimmune maintenance.

## Masked antibody
Potential future option for organ-localized disease, but masking a bispecific inhibitory geometry increases developability risk. Only revisit after base format is strong.

## pH-dependent recycling
Low priority. These are membrane co-ligation targets where geometry and residence time matter more than soluble antigen clearance.
