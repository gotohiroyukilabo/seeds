# Seed Proposal 1: CD19 x FcGR2B Inhibitory Bispecific

## One-sentence proposal
Develop a Fc-silent CD19 x FcGR2B bispecific antibody that inhibits autoreactive B cells and plasmablasts without cytotoxic depletion, first in IgG4-RD relapse prevention or B-cell-high systemic Sjögren disease.

## Disease wall
Steroids and B-cell depletion work but relapse, infection and hypogammaglobulinemia remain. Chronic diseases need maintenance control that is less destructive than repeated depletion.

## Target validity
CD19 marks a broad B-lineage/plasmablast compartment, while FcGR2B is an inhibitory receptor. Obexelimab’s active phase 2/3 landscape supports clinical plausibility, but also creates competitive pressure.

## Why antibody is necessary
The therapeutic concept requires spatially controlled co-engagement, not simple dual target occupancy. CD19 localizes the molecule to B-lineage cells and the BCR/CD19 co-receptor activation context, while FcGR2B provides an ITIM/SHIP inhibitory brake. A bispecific can physically bridge CD19 and FcGR2B on the same B cell, raising the activation threshold and suppressing BCR-driven calcium signaling, antigen uptake, antigen presentation, T-cell help, germinal-center biology and plasmablast/antibody responses. Separate anti-CD19 and anti-FcGR2B antibodies would not reliably create this same cis co-engagement geometry.

## Antibody design
Fc-silent, non-cytolytic bispecific. Avoid ADCC/CDC. Optimize geometry for inhibitory signaling rather than depletion.

Key design hypothesis: the molecule should behave as a synthetic inhibitory immune complex on CD19-positive B cells, without triggering cytotoxic depletion or broad FcγR activation on non-B cells.

## Differentiation
The base CD19 x FcGR2B thesis is not differentiated from obexelimab. A new seed is only viable if it deliberately avoids being an obexelimab follower. Potentially credible wedges are: a different bispecific geometry with demonstrably stronger inhibition and less B-cell sequestration; subcutaneous low-burden maintenance dosing; a disease segment not prioritized by obexelimab; or biomarker-selected BCR/GC-high patients where obexelimab has not established positioning. IgG4-RD is a poor first wedge because obexelimab is phase 3 and CD19 depletion with inebilizumab/Uplizna is already approved.

## Major risks
Insufficient efficacy in organ-threatening disease; no meaningful differentiation versus obexelimab; FTO versus Xencor/Zenas intellectual property; target-density dependence; unexpected FcγR biology. The default recommendation is not to advance unless a concrete differentiation wedge is proven experimentally or strategically.

## First validation experiment
Use IgG4-RD and Sjögren patient PBMC/plasmablast samples to test BCR signaling, plasmablast differentiation, Ig secretion and cytokine activation with non-depleting controls.

Critical mechanistic readouts: FcGR2B ITIM phosphorylation, SHIP pathway engagement, reduced BCR-induced Ca flux, reduced BCR-mediated antigen uptake, reduced B-cell antigen presentation to T cells, and preserved non-depleting profile.

## Go / No-Go
Go only if the molecule suppresses patient B-cell activation and Ig production at feasible exposure without depletion/cytokine activation and shows a measurable advantage over an obexelimab-like benchmark. No-Go if inhibition is merely comparable, requires depletion, cannot avoid obexelimab's IP/clinical lane, or needs IgG4-RD as the lead indication.
