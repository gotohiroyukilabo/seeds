# Seed Proposal 3: Fc-Silent or Masked Anti-OSMR

## One-sentence proposal
Develop a Fc-silent OSMR-blocking antibody, with a masked version as a safety-widening backup, for OSMR-high fibrotic skin or HS-like remodeling disease.

## Disease wall
Fibrotic remodeling, tunnels, itch/pain and tissue damage often persist despite cytokine or B-cell immunomodulation. Broad immunosuppression does not directly address stromal disease programs.

## Target validity
OSMR is a membrane receptor on stromal/epithelial compartments and plausibly controls inflammatory remodeling. The target is less clinically crowded, but human causal evidence must be built.

## Why antibody is necessary
Receptor blockade with Fc control and potential masking is best achieved by antibody engineering. Small molecules would likely be less selective for this receptor complex.

## Antibody design
Start with Fc-silent blocking IgG. Add protease-activated masking if normal tissue repair assays show a narrow safety window.

## Differentiation
Moves beyond immune-cell suppression toward tissue remodeling. Could pair with lesion transcriptomics for patient selection.

## Major risks
OSMR may be a marker, not driver; impaired wound healing/epithelial repair; uncertain translational species; no established clinical precedent.

## First validation experiment
Run OSM-stimulated fibroblast, keratinocyte and disease explant assays from SSc/HS lesions, alongside wound repair safety assays.

## Go / No-Go
Go if OSMR-high patient tissue shows reversible inflammatory/fibrotic signatures without unacceptable repair impairment. No-Go if blockade does not alter patient tissue biology or normal repair is strongly suppressed.
