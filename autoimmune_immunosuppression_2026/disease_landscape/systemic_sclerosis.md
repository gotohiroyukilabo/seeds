# Systemic Sclerosis

## Thesis
Systemic sclerosis is high-risk but strategically interesting: current therapy still leans on immunosuppression and anti-fibrotic slowing, while antibody targets may address immune-fibrotic coupling in selected early diffuse or inflammatory-fibrotic patients.

## Unmet need
- Skin fibrosis, ILD progression, vascular disease and organ damage remain difficult to reverse.
- Steroids are limited by renal-crisis risk; MMF, cyclophosphamide, rituximab/tocilizumab/nintedanib are partial solutions depending organ domain.
- Need is strongest in early diffuse cutaneous disease and progressive ILD/fibrosis despite SOC.

## Antibody-accessible biology
- CCL24/CCR3 axis links eosinophil/monocyte trafficking and fibrosis.
- OSMR mediates stromal, epithelial and fibroblast remodeling signals.
- IL-6R and B-cell pathways are validated but crowded.
- TL1A may bridge mucosal/vascular inflammation and fibrosis, but direct SSc evidence is less mature.

## Biomarker strategy
Modified Rodnan skin score trajectory, FVC/DLCO, HRCT, CRP, CCL24, eosinophils, skin RNA/fibroblast signatures, autoantibodies such as anti-topoisomerase I and RNA polymerase III.

## Competition
Moderate. IL-6R/B-cell approaches are known; CCL24 and OSMR are less crowded but carry biology risk.

## Seed-relevant targets
CCL24, OSMR, BAFF-R, IL6R, TL1A.
