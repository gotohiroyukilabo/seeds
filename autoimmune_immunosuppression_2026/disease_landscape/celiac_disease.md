# Celiac Disease / Refractory or Nonresponsive Disease

## Thesis
Nonresponsive and refractory celiac disease are attractive only if narrowed to immune-active, steroid/budesonide-dependent populations. Antibody opportunities include IL-15 and costimulation pathways, but routine celiac disease is not an antibody-seed fit.

## Unmet need
- Gluten-free diet is burdensome and incomplete for some.
- Refractory celiac disease may require steroids, immunosuppressants or cladribine-like approaches; RCD-II carries lymphoma risk.

## Antibody-accessible biology
- IL-15 supports intraepithelial lymphocyte activation and survival in refractory disease.
- OX40L/CD40L/TL1A-like costimulation could modulate mucosal T-cell activation.
- Amlitelimab/OX40L trial in nonresponsive celiac validates active industry interest.

## Biomarker strategy
Histology, villous height/crypt depth, IEL phenotype, gluten exposure, serology, IL-15 pathway markers, T-cell clonality for RCD-II.

## Competition
Medium. Many oral enzyme/barrier/tolerance approaches exist; systemic antibodies must justify safety for a diet-managed disease, likely by focusing on severe refractory disease.

## Seed-relevant targets
IL15, OX40L, TL1A, IL7R, CD40L.
