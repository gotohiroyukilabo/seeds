# SLE / Lupus Nephritis

## Thesis
High unmet need remains in patients who are steroid-, MMF-, calcineurin-inhibitor- or biologic-dependent. Antibody seeds are most plausible in biomarker-defined B-cell, type I IFN, immune-complex or T-B costimulation subsets.

## Why this is still immunosuppression-dependent
- Current standard therapy often combines glucocorticoids with hydroxychloroquine, MMF, cyclophosphamide, calcineurin inhibitors, belimumab or anifrolumab.
- Renal response, flare prevention and steroid taper remain incomplete, especially in proliferative lupus nephritis and multi-organ SLE.
- Unknown: exact size of the high-steroid-dependent, biologic-refractory segment by molecular endotype.

## Antibody-accessible biology
- CD40L-CD40 controls T-cell help, B-cell activation and APC activation.
- BAFF/APRIL/BAFF-R support autoreactive B-cell and plasma-cell survival.
- FcRn blockade can lower pathogenic IgG without direct B-cell depletion.
- IFNAR1 blockade is already validated by anifrolumab, but renal and high-risk subgroups remain a question.

## Biomarker strategy
- Anti-dsDNA, complement C3/C4, proteinuria, urinary biomarkers, IFN gene signature, plasmablast/BAFF/CXCL13 levels.
- Patient selection should separate IFN-high, B-cell-high, immune-complex/FcRn-high and fibrotic renal chronicity-high groups.

## Competitive density
High. Belimumab, anifrolumab, voclosporin, anti-CD20/CD19 approaches, CD40L programs and FcRn programs create crowding. A new seed needs either safer CD40L, molecularly selected FcRn/B-cell modulation or tissue-targeted design.

## Seed-relevant targets
CD40L, BAFF-R, FcRn, APRIL, CD19-FcGR2B, IFNAR1.
