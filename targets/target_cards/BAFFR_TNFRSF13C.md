# Target Card: BAFF receptor / TNFRSF13C

## 1. Summary
- Disease area: Autoimmune disease, B-cell-driven autoimmunity
- Indication / patient segment: Sjogren disease, SLE/LN subsets, autoimmune cytopenias, autoimmune hepatitis; especially autoantibody-high or B-cell-activation-high patients
- Target: BAFF receptor / TNFRSF13C
- UniProt ID: Q96RJ3
- Location: Membrane protein on B cells
- Proposed modality: Fc-engineered or Fc-silent anti-BAFF-R antibody
- Proposed MoA: BAFF-R blockade with optional depletion of BAFF-R-positive B cells
- Recommendation: High
- One-line thesis: BAFF-R is a B-cell-selective membrane target that may differentiate from soluble BAFF blockade by directly targeting BAFF-dependent autoreactive B cells.
- Main reason to advance: Strong B-cell biology and clinical validation signal from ianalumab-class agents.
- Main reason to stop: Competitive programs are active, and infection/hypogammaglobulinemia risk may limit chronic autoimmune use.
- Last literature / landscape check date: 2026-07-05

## 3. Disease rationale
- Unmet medical need: Sjogren disease and SLE/LN have residual activity, flares, steroid burden, fatigue/systemic disease, and incomplete response to existing biologics.
- Patient segment: Autoantibody-positive, hypergammaglobulinemic, B-cell-activation-high patients.
- Current standard of care: Immunosuppressants, steroids, belimumab/anifrolumab in lupus; no broadly established disease-modifying Sjogren therapy.
- Remaining problem: Heterogeneous response and limited durable disease modification.

## 4. Target biology
- Protein function: Receptor for BAFF/TNFSF13B; supports B-cell maturation and survival.
- Expression pattern: Mainly B-lineage cells; exact disease tissue distribution requires confirmation.
- Disease relevance: BAFF axis is linked to autoreactive B-cell survival in SLE/Sjogren biology.
- Human genetic evidence: TNFRSF13C variants can affect humoral immunity; direct autoimmune causal genetics are not fully established.
- Omics evidence: B-cell/BAFF signatures reported across autoimmune diseases; disease-specific strength varies.
- Causal strength: Moderate / Strong depending disease.

## 5. Why antibody?
- Is the target extracellular or membrane-bound? Membrane-bound receptor with extracellular ligand-binding domain.
- Required antibody function:
  - Blocking
  - Depletion
- Why not small molecule / RNA / protein therapy? Cell-surface receptor biology and optional Fc-mediated depletion favor antibody design.
- Antibody-specific advantage: Can tune blockade versus depletion through Fc engineering.
- Antibody-specific liability: Long-lasting B-cell effects may reduce safety flexibility.

## 6. Existing landscape
- Approved drugs: No approved anti-BAFF-R antibody confirmed.
- Clinical-stage agents: Ianalumab/VAY736.
- Key competitors: BAFF/APRIL axis agents including belimumab and ligand traps.
- Differentiation opportunity: BAFF-R-specific B-cell targeting in biomarker-enriched Sjogren/SLE subsets.
- Differentiation weakness, if any: If ianalumab becomes standard, follow-on differentiation may narrow.

## 7. Antibody design hypothesis
- Format: Fc-silent IgG for blockade or Fc-enabled IgG for partial depletion.
- Epitope hypothesis: Ligand-blocking epitope on extracellular BAFF-binding domain.
- Desired affinity: High enough for durable receptor occupancy; exact target KD unknown.
- Fc function: Disease-dependent; Fc-silent may improve chronic safety, Fc-active may improve depletion.
- Species cross-reactivity: Must be checked; non-human primate binding likely important.
- Biomarker strategy: BAFF, B-cell subsets, IgG, autoantibodies, complement, salivary gland or tissue B-cell signatures.

## 8. Risks
- Biology risk: B-cell depletion/blockade may not translate to symptom improvement in heterogeneous Sjogren.
- Clinical history risk: BAFF-axis drugs have shown pathway validation but also efficacy failures in unselected autoimmune populations; tabalumab and atacicept are cautionary comparators.
- Safety risk: Infection, low immunoglobulins, vaccine impairment; broader BAFF/APRIL blockade has shown immune-balance risks in some contexts.
- Redundancy risk: APRIL, CD40L, IL-21, IFN, and tissue survival niches may maintain autoantibody biology despite BAFF-R blockade.
- Patient selection risk: Need B-cell-driven subgroup; all-comer Sjogren/SLE designs may dilute effect.
- Biomarker risk: BAFF/B-cell PD changes may not translate into clinical benefit.
- Dosing / PK risk: Durable receptor occupancy and B-cell modulation may require chronic exposure with a narrow safety margin.
- Immunogenicity risk: Unknown; chronic autoimmune dosing requires ADA monitoring.
- Developability risk: Receptor internalization and Fc function need optimization.
- Competitive risk: Ianalumab and BAFF/APRIL programs.

## 9. Key experiments and kill criteria
- In vitro assay: BAFF-induced B-cell survival assay with human B cells.
- Cell assay: B-cell subset depletion/blockade profiling.
- Disease model: Human ex vivo Sjogren/SLE B-cell cultures; animal model relevance limited.
- Biomarker assay: BAFF, plasmablasts, IgG, autoantibody reduction.
- Developability assay: Receptor occupancy, internalization, Fc effector function, aggregation.
- Kill criteria: No selective activity in autoreactive/B-cell-activation-high samples; no superiority versus BAFF ligand blockade; clinical PD changes do not map to disease-relevant readouts; unacceptable broad B-cell toxicity, hypogammaglobulinemia, infection signal, or impractical receptor occupancy exposure.

## 11. Final judgment
- Strengths: Strong B-cell biology; antibody tractable; biomarkers available.
- Weaknesses: Competition and immune suppression.
- Differentiation summary: Selective receptor targeting and Fc tuning.
- Antibody rationale summary: Membrane receptor enables blockade and optional depletion.
- Kill criteria: No clear superiority versus BAFF/APRIL ligand blockade, no responder-enrichment strategy, or early Ig/infection safety signal.
- Next action: Deep-dive ianalumab clinical data and Sjogren biomarker-defined responder groups.
