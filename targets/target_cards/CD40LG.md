# Target Card: CD40 ligand / CD154 / TNFSF5

## 1. Summary
- Disease area: Autoimmune disease with T-B cell costimulation
- Indication / patient segment: SLE/LN, Sjogren disease, dermatomyositis, MS-adjacent autoimmune biology
- Target: CD40 ligand / CD154 / TNFSF5
- UniProt ID: P29965
- Location: Membrane and soluble ligand, mainly activated T cells and platelets
- Proposed modality: Fc-silent anti-CD40L antibody, Fab, or non-platelet-activating format
- Proposed MoA: Block CD40L-CD40 costimulation to suppress B-cell help and antigen-presenting-cell activation
- Recommendation: High
- One-line thesis: CD40L is a central autoimmune costimulation node with renewed feasibility through safer next-generation antibody formats.
- Main reason to advance: Strong biology across autoantibody diseases and multiple active clinical programs.
- Main reason to stop: Historical thrombosis risk and broad immune suppression.
- Last literature / landscape check date: 2026-07-05

## 3. Disease rationale
- Unmet medical need: Autoantibody-driven diseases need steroid-sparing, durable control.
- Patient segment: T-B costimulation-high, plasmablast-high, autoantibody-high disease.
- Current standard of care: Steroids, immunosuppressants, B-cell/IFN biologics depending disease.
- Remaining problem: Incomplete response, flares, toxicity.

## 4. Target biology
- Protein function: CD40L binds CD40 and drives B-cell class switching, germinal centers, APC activation.
- Expression pattern: Activated CD4 T cells; also platelets and soluble forms.
- Disease relevance: T-B interaction is core to autoantibody generation.
- Causal strength: Strong mechanistic biology; disease-specific clinical proof varies.

## 5. Why antibody?
- Is the target extracellular or membrane-bound? Extracellular ligand accessible to antibodies.
- Required antibody function:
  - Blocking
- Why not small molecule / RNA / protein therapy? Protein-protein interaction blockade and soluble/membrane ligand neutralization suit biologics.
- Antibody-specific advantage: High specificity and long exposure.
- Antibody-specific liability: Fc-mediated platelet crosslinking risk if poorly designed.

## 6. Existing landscape
- Approved drugs: No approved CD40L blocker for autoimmune disease confirmed.
- Clinical-stage agents: Dapirolizumab pegol, frexalimab, dazodalibep and related agents.
- Failed programs: Earlier anti-CD40L antibodies encountered thrombotic safety issues.
- Differentiation opportunity: Platelet-sparing format, disease-specific biomarker enrichment, convenient dosing.
- Differentiation weakness, if any: Active competitors are already testing the renewed hypothesis.

## 7. Antibody design hypothesis
- Format: Fc-silent IgG, Fab/PEG-Fab, or engineered non-crosslinking antibody.
- Epitope hypothesis: Block CD40 binding without platelet activation.
- Desired affinity: High ligand occupancy; exact KD unknown.
- Fc function: Avoid activating Fc gamma receptor engagement.
- Species cross-reactivity: NHP relevance important for platelet/thrombosis assessment.
- Biomarker strategy: Plasmablasts, immunoglobulins, autoantibody titers, CD40L/CD40 pathway gene signatures.

## 8. Risks
- Biology risk: Broad upstream blockade may not improve all endpoints.
- Clinical history risk: Earlier anti-CD40L programs encountered thromboembolic safety issues; newer formats must not be evaluated as if this history disappeared.
- Safety risk: Thrombosis, platelet activation, infection, impaired vaccine response.
- Redundancy risk: Established plasma cells, innate inflammation, IFN-high disease, and tissue-resident autoimmunity may persist despite CD40L blockade.
- Patient selection risk: Broad SLE/Sjogren populations may dilute effect if not T-B costimulation-driven.
- Biomarker risk: T-B costimulation PD markers may not translate to composite clinical endpoints.
- Dosing / PK risk: Fab/PEG or Fc-silent formats may trade lower thrombosis risk for exposure, dosing-frequency, or tissue-penetration limitations.
- Immunogenicity risk: PEGylated, fragment, or engineered formats require ADA monitoring.
- CMC / developability risk: Aggregate control, valency, Fc silence, and immune-complex behavior are safety-critical.
- Competitive risk: Several advanced CD40L programs.
- Evidence quality risk: Must separate new-format data from historical class toxicity.

## 9. Key experiments and kill criteria
- In vitro assay: CD40L-CD40 blocking assay; T-B co-culture Ig production.
- Cell assay: Platelet activation/crosslinking assay.
- Disease model: Human autoimmune PBMC and organoid/ex vivo tissue systems.
- Biomarker assay: Plasmablast and autoantibody reduction.
- Developability assay: Fc silence, aggregation, soluble CD40L immune complex behavior.
- Kill criteria: Any platelet activation, platelet aggregation, FcγR/C1q binding, coagulation marker, or immune-complex safety signal; weak T-B blockade versus competitors; no biomarker-defined responder strategy; ADA or PK issue that prevents sustained costimulation blockade.

## 11. Final judgment
- Strengths: Strong biology, broad autoimmune relevance.
- Weaknesses: Safety history and competition.
- Differentiation summary: Non-platelet-activating format plus biomarker-defined disease.
- Antibody rationale summary: Ligand blockade is best served by biologic specificity.
- Kill criteria: Platelet activation, thrombosis signals, no differentiation from active programs, or inability to prove a non-thrombogenic format.
- Next action: Map CD40L clinical assets by format, Fc design, indication, and safety events.
