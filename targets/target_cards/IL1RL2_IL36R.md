# Target Card: IL-36 receptor / IL1RL2

## 1. Summary
- Disease area: Neutrophilic and epithelial inflammatory autoimmune/immune-mediated skin disease
- Indication / patient segment: Hidradenitis suppurativa, pustular/neutrophilic skin disease, selected autoimmune blistering/inflammatory skin subsets
- Target: IL-36 receptor / IL1RL2
- UniProt ID: Q9HB29
- Location: Membrane cytokine receptor
- Proposed modality: Anti-IL-36R blocking antibody
- Proposed MoA: Block IL-36 cytokine signaling and downstream epithelial-neutrophil inflammation
- Recommendation: High
- One-line thesis: IL-36R is validated in pustular skin inflammation and may be extendable to IL-36-high autoimmune skin subsets with less crowding than TNF/IL-17.
- Main reason to advance: Antibody tractability and skin-biomarker feasibility.
- Main reason to stop: Relevance outside generalized pustular psoriasis is still uncertain.
- Last literature / landscape check date: 2026-07-05

## 3. Disease rationale
- Unmet medical need: HS and neutrophilic skin diseases have incomplete response to TNF/IL-17 blockade, pain, drainage, scarring, and relapse.
- Patient segment: IL-36 pathway-high lesional skin, neutrophil-rich disease.
- Current standard of care: Antibiotics, surgery, TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors depending disease.
- Remaining problem: Nonresponse, tunnels/scarring, chronic relapsing inflammation.

## 4. Target biology
- Protein function: Receptor for IL-36 cytokines, activating inflammatory epithelial and innate immune pathways.
- Expression pattern: Epithelial/immune cells; exact HS subset expression requires confirmation.
- Disease relevance: Strong in pustular psoriasis; hypothesized in broader neutrophilic skin inflammation.
- Causal strength: Strong in GPP; Moderate/Weak in HS until validated.

## 5. Why antibody?
- Is the target extracellular or membrane-bound? Membrane receptor with extracellular domain.
- Required antibody function:
  - Blocking
- Why not small molecule / RNA / protein therapy? Receptor-ligand blockade has existing antibody feasibility.
- Antibody-specific advantage: High pathway specificity and long dosing interval.
- Antibody-specific liability: May miss intracellular/parallel inflammasome drivers.

## 6. Existing landscape
- Approved drugs: Spesolimab anti-IL-36R approved for generalized pustular psoriasis flares.
- Clinical-stage agents: Other IL-36 pathway blockers in dermatology.
- Key competitors: TNF and IL-17 agents in HS; IL-36 agents in pustular psoriasis.
- Differentiation opportunity: Biomarker-selected HS/neutrophilic skin disease where TNF/IL-17 response is inadequate.
- Differentiation weakness, if any: Approved IL-36R precedent limits novelty if same indication.

## 7. Antibody design hypothesis
- Format: Blocking IgG, likely Fc-silent if receptor crosslinking risk exists.
- Epitope hypothesis: Ligand-binding domain blockade.
- Desired affinity: High receptor occupancy in inflamed skin.
- Fc function: Likely not needed.
- Biomarker strategy: Lesional IL36A/B/G, IL36RN status where relevant, neutrophil signatures, keratinocyte activation markers.

## 8. Risks
- Biology risk: HS may not be IL-36-dependent in enough patients; GPP validation may not translate to broader autoimmune skin disease.
- Clinical history risk: Direct IL-36R success is strongest in generalized pustular psoriasis; extension indications remain uncertain.
- Safety risk: Infection or impaired epithelial barrier responses, especially with chronic dosing.
- Redundancy risk: TNF, IL-17, IL-1, IL-23, complement, microbiome, and structural tunnel/scarring biology may bypass IL-36R blockade.
- Patient selection risk: Need robust skin biomarker assay and IL-36 pathway-high enrichment.
- Biomarker risk: IL36A/B/G expression may be a marker rather than a response predictor unless functional blockade is shown.
- Dosing / PK risk: Skin lesion penetration and sustained receptor occupancy are uncertain in HS/chronic disease.
- Immunogenicity risk: Unknown for novel formats.
- Competitive risk: Spesolimab-class agents.

## 9. Key experiments and kill criteria
- In vitro assay: IL-36-induced cytokine release in keratinocytes.
- Cell assay: HS lesional explant cytokine suppression.
- Disease model: Human skin explants preferred; animal translation limited.
- Biomarker assay: IL-36 pathway RNA/protein in lesions.
- Developability assay: Receptor blockade, internalization, aggregation.
- Kill criteria: No IL-36-high HS/autoimmune skin subset; IL-36R blockade fails to suppress lesional inflammatory signatures; no advantage versus TNF/IL-17 comparators in ex vivo tissue; chronic infection/barrier safety signal; tissue exposure insufficient for receptor occupancy.

## 11. Final judgment
- Strengths: Accessible receptor, clinical precedent, biomarker-friendly tissue.
- Weaknesses: Disease extension risk.
- Differentiation summary: Move beyond IL-17/TNF with IL-36-high patient selection.
- Antibody rationale summary: Receptor blockade is direct and proven feasible.
- Kill criteria: No clear IL-36-dependent autoimmune skin subgroup, no functional lesional-tissue suppression, or no differentiation from existing dermatology biologics.
- Next action: Compare IL-36 pathway expression in HS, BP, pustular psoriasis, and control skin datasets.
