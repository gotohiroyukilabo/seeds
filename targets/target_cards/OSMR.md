# Target Card: OSM receptor / OSMR

## 1. Summary
- Disease area: Chronic inflammatory skin disease and fibrotic autoimmune disease
- Indication / patient segment: Hidradenitis suppurativa with scarring/remodeling, prurigo/atopic overlap, systemic sclerosis or fibrotic inflammatory subsets
- Target: Oncostatin M receptor / OSMR
- UniProt ID: Q99650
- Location: Membrane cytokine receptor
- Proposed modality: Anti-OSMR blocking antibody
- Proposed MoA: Block OSM-family stromal/epithelial inflammatory remodeling and possibly itch-related signaling depending epitope
- Recommendation: High
- One-line thesis: OSMR may address chronic tissue remodeling and inflammation, a residual problem not fully covered by TNF/IL-17/B-cell axes.
- Main reason to advance: Differentiated biology around stromal activation, fibrosis, itch, and chronic lesions.
- Main reason to stop: Autoimmune causal evidence is weaker than for B-cell or classic cytokine targets.
- Last literature / landscape check date: 2026-07-05

## 3. Disease rationale
- Unmet medical need: Chronic skin and fibrotic autoimmune diseases have residual scarring, remodeling, itch, and tissue dysfunction despite anti-inflammatory treatment.
- Patient segment: OSM/OSMR-high lesional tissue, stromal activation-high disease.
- Current standard of care: TNF/IL-17 for HS, immunosuppression/anti-fibrotics for systemic sclerosis-related disease.
- Remaining problem: Structural damage and chronic nonresolving lesions.

## 4. Target biology
- Protein function: Receptor for IL-6 family cytokine signaling involving OSM and related pathways.
- Expression pattern: Stromal/epithelial/immune-context expression; exact disease distribution must be confirmed.
- Disease relevance: OSM-family signaling is associated with inflammation, itch, epithelial dysfunction, and tissue remodeling.
- Causal strength: Moderate in skin/itch contexts; Weak/Moderate in systemic sclerosis until validated.

## 5. Why antibody?
- Is the target extracellular or membrane-bound? Membrane receptor.
- Required antibody function:
  - Blocking
- Why not small molecule / RNA / protein therapy? Receptor-ligand interface and tissue cytokine biology fit antibody blockade.
- Antibody-specific advantage: Selective pathway blockade and durable exposure.
- Antibody-specific liability: Blocking receptor may affect multiple related cytokine signals.

## 6. Existing landscape
- Approved drugs: No approved OSMR antibody in autoimmune disease confirmed.
- Clinical-stage agents: Vixarelimab anti-OSMR reported in dermatology development.
- Key competitors: TNF/IL-17/IL-4R/IL-31 axis agents depending indication.
- Differentiation opportunity: Remodeling/scarring or itch-dominant disease segments.
- Differentiation weakness, if any: Disease causality remains less established.

## 7. Antibody design hypothesis
- Format: Blocking IgG, likely Fc-silent.
- Epitope hypothesis: Block OSM binding or receptor complex formation.
- Desired affinity: High receptor occupancy in inflamed tissue.
- Fc function: Not required.
- Biomarker strategy: OSM, OSMR, stromal activation genes, fibrosis markers, lesion histology.

## 8. Risks
- Biology risk: OSMR may be associated marker rather than driver; autoimmune/fibrotic causal evidence is less mature than B-cell or classic cytokine targets.
- Clinical history risk: Vixarelimab provides OSMR antibody tractability, but no approved OSMR autoimmune/fibrotic drug was confirmed; direct failure history is limited, which lowers evidence maturity.
- Safety risk: Wound healing, epithelial repair, infection susceptibility, and barrier response effects are uncertain.
- Redundancy risk: IL-6, IL-13, TGF-beta activation, IL-31, TNF, IL-17, and tissue damage loops may compensate.
- Patient selection risk: Requires OSM/OSMR-high tissue biomarker strategy.
- Biomarker risk: OSMR expression may not equal causal dependence; functional ex vivo blockade is needed.
- Dosing / PK risk: Fibrotic/tunnelled lesions may have poor antibody penetration.
- Immunogenicity risk: Unknown for novel formats/bispecifics.
- CMC / developability risk: Epitope must define whether OSM-only or broader receptor-family signaling is blocked.
- Competitive risk: Dermatology space has many cytokine antibodies.

## 9. Key experiments and kill criteria
- In vitro assay: OSM-induced fibroblast/keratinocyte inflammatory and remodeling readouts.
- Cell assay: HS or SSc tissue explant suppression of stromal signatures.
- Disease model: Human tissue models prioritized.
- Biomarker assay: OSM/OSMR protein and RNA in lesions.
- Developability assay: Receptor occupancy, ligand competition, cross-reactivity.
- Kill criteria: No OSMR-high disease segment; OSMR expression is not linked to functional dependence; no causal suppression of remodeling/fibrotic signatures in human disease tissue; repair/wound-healing readouts worsen; tissue exposure is inadequate.

## 11. Final judgment
- Strengths: Differentiated tissue-remodeling hypothesis; antibody tractable.
- Weaknesses: Causality and safety less certain.
- Differentiation summary: Focus on chronic remodeling, not just acute inflammation.
- Antibody rationale summary: Membrane receptor blockade is feasible.
- Kill criteria: No OSMR-high disease segment, no ex vivo tissue response, or unacceptable repair/wound-healing liability.
- Next action: Mine public transcriptomic datasets for OSM/OSMR-high autoimmune skin/fibrotic subsets.
