# Target Card: CCL24 / eotaxin-2

## 1. Summary
- Disease area: Fibrotic and eosinophil/myeloid-inflamed autoimmune disease
- Indication / patient segment: Systemic sclerosis, autoimmune liver disease/PSC-like fibrosis, eosinophil-associated inflammatory autoimmune subsets
- Target: CCL24 / eotaxin-2
- UniProt ID: O00175
- Location: Secreted chemokine
- Proposed modality: Anti-CCL24 neutralizing antibody
- Proposed MoA: Neutralize CCL24 to reduce CCR3-mediated eosinophil/myeloid recruitment and fibrotic inflammatory signaling
- Recommendation: High
- One-line thesis: CCL24 is a less crowded secreted chemokine target with a plausible inflammation-fibrosis bridge in systemic sclerosis and related diseases.
- Main reason to advance: Lower competitive density and potential fibrotic autoimmune positioning.
- Main reason to stop: Human causal evidence and patient selection are still uncertain.

## 3. Disease rationale
- Unmet medical need: Systemic sclerosis and autoimmune liver fibrotic diseases lack therapies that reliably reverse fibrosis.
- Patient segment: CCL24-high, eosinophil/myeloid-inflamed, progressive fibrotic disease.
- Current standard of care: Immunosuppression and anti-fibrotics depending disease; efficacy remains limited.
- Remaining problem: Fibrosis progression, organ dysfunction, and lack of precision-targeted therapies.

## 4. Target biology
- Protein function: Secreted CC chemokine that binds CCR3 and attracts eosinophils; may affect inflammatory/fibrotic circuits.
- Expression pattern: Secreted by inflammatory/stromal contexts; disease-specific tissue sources need confirmation.
- Disease relevance: Reported/claimed relevance in fibrotic inflammatory diseases including systemic sclerosis programs.
- Causal strength: Moderate/Weak until human interventional data mature.

## 5. Why antibody?
- Is the target extracellular or membrane-bound? Secreted extracellular chemokine.
- Required antibody function:
  - Blocking
- Why not small molecule / RNA / protein therapy? Neutralizing a soluble chemokine is a classic antibody use case.
- Antibody-specific advantage: High specificity and long duration.
- Antibody-specific liability: Chemokine redundancy may limit efficacy.

## 6. Existing landscape
- Approved drugs: No approved anti-CCL24 antibody confirmed.
- Clinical-stage agents: CM-101/anti-CCL24 reported in fibrotic/inflammatory disease development.
- Key competitors: IL-6, TGF-beta/integrin, B-cell, anti-fibrotic small molecule strategies.
- Differentiation opportunity: Fibrosis plus inflammation positioning in less crowded target space.
- Differentiation weakness, if any: Mechanistic validation still thinner than mainstream immune targets.

## 7. Antibody design hypothesis
- Format: Neutralizing IgG.
- Epitope hypothesis: Block CCR3-binding interface.
- Desired affinity: High ligand neutralization under inflammatory concentrations.
- Fc function: Not required.
- Biomarker strategy: CCL24 levels, CCR3+ cells, eosinophils, fibrotic serum markers, skin/liver/lung tissue signatures.

## 8. Risks
- Biology risk: Chemokine redundancy; CCL24 may be marker not driver.
- Clinical history risk: No confirmed direct anti-CCL24 efficacy failure was found in quick public scan, but this mainly means evidence maturity is low.
- Safety risk: Eosinophil trafficking, host defense, allergy balance, and tissue-repair effects.
- Redundancy risk: CCL11/eotaxin-1 and CCL26/eotaxin-3 may compensate through CCR3; fibrosis has multiple parallel mediators.
- Patient selection risk: Need CCL24-high, CCR3-cell-rich, progressive fibrotic subgroup.
- Biomarker risk: Serum CCL24 may not reflect tissue causal biology.
- Dosing / PK risk: Secreted chemokine antigen sink may require high exposure; tissue concentration and turnover are uncertain.
- Immunogenicity risk: Unknown for engineered or pH-dependent recycling designs.
- CMC / developability risk: pH-dependent designs may add stability, affinity, and manufacturability risk.
- Competitive risk: CM-101-like programs may occupy the space.

## 9. Key experiments and kill criteria
- In vitro assay: CCL24-CCR3 chemotaxis blockade.
- Cell assay: Fibroblast/eosinophil/myeloid co-culture readouts.
- Disease model: Human SSc skin/lung explants; liver organoid/fibrosis models.
- Biomarker assay: CCL24 protein/RNA and CCR3+ cell infiltration.
- Developability assay: Ligand binding, neutralization potency, aggregation.
- Kill criteria: No elevated CCL24 in target patient tissue; CCL24 neutralization has no effect on fibrotic inflammatory readouts; CCL11/CCL26 redundancy bypasses blockade; antigen sink makes practical dosing implausible; no biomarker strategy beyond serum CCL24.

## 11. Final judgment
- Strengths: Less crowded, secreted target, fibrotic autoimmune fit.
- Weaknesses: Causal evidence and biomarker strategy need work.
- Differentiation summary: Fibrosis/inflammation niche outside crowded B-cell/cytokine targets.
- Antibody rationale summary: Neutralizing soluble chemokine is straightforward.
- Kill criteria: Weak disease tissue expression, redundant chemokine compensation, no functional neutralization effect, or impractical PK/antigen-sink profile.
